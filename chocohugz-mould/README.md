# ChocoHugz® Signature Chocolate Mould: 3D files

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `chocohugz_6in_mould.stl` / `chocohugz_8in_mould.stl` | Ready-to-print mould. Pour the chocolate straight in. |
| `chocohugz_6in_master.stl` / `chocohugz_8in_master.stl` | The chocolate shape itself, used as a master for casting a silicone mould. |
| `chocohugz_6in_pour_box.stl` / `chocohugz_8in_pour_box.stl` | Cup that holds the master while you pour the silicone. |
| `make_mould.py` | Parametric generator: change the sizes, text, flute count or relief here and re-run it. |

**Dimensions:** 6" = 152.4 mm diameter. 8" = 203.2 mm diameter. Both are 38.1 mm (1.5") deep, with a 2° draft and a 1.2 mm logo relief (1.6 mm on the 8").
A solid 6" disc holds about 670 cm³ (about 870 g of chocolate). The 8" holds about 1200 cm³ (about 1.5 kg).

## Recommended: food-grade silicone mould (what the photo shows)
1. Print `master` face-up in PLA at a 0.12 mm layer height for crisp lettering. Sand it lightly and seal it with clear lacquer or epoxy.
2. Glue the master into the locating pocket of `pour_box` with a dab of hot glue.
3. Pour food-grade platinum-cure silicone (Shore 20–30A, for example a pink-tinted one). That gives about 10 mm of silicone all round.
4. Let it cure, then pull out the master. You now have a flexible, food-safe mould that releases cleanly.

## Direct-print mould
Print it flange-down with no supports. Use a 0.12–0.16 mm layer height and 4 walls.
Plain PLA/PETG prints are **not** food-safe because their layer lines trap bacteria. Use the printed mould for prototypes, or coat it with a food-safe epoxy before using it with food.

The logo uses the Pacifico (script) and Montserrat fonts as stand-ins. For the exact brand lettering, provide the logo as an SVG and it can be swapped in.
